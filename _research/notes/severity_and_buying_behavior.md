# Severity of pain and buying behaviour of Nigerian independent artists (as of Nov 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-20. About 25 tool calls across web search and fetch. Coverage limits: no Reddit, Nairaland or X thread text was retrievable. Upwork returned an error. No verifiable buyer testimonials were found. Where I convert currencies, I label it as my own calculation.

FX reference for 2026 conversions: the official NFEM rate was about ₦1,351/$ on 10 Nov 2026 and the parallel market was about ₦1,430-1,440/$ on 12 Nov 2026 ([Within Nigeria, 12 Nov 2026](https://www.withinnigeria.com/2026/11/12/dollar-to-naira-today-november-12-2026-nfem-and-cbn-rate-near-%E2%82%A61350-as-black-market-hits-%E2%82%A61430/?amp=1)). The same source says the naira is about 6.3% stronger than on 6 Nov 2025. The Spotify 2025 payout figures imply about ₦1,366/$.

At the official rate, the client's prices work out to roughly: ₦35,000 = about $26, and ₦99,700 = about $74 (my calculation).

## 1. What do Nigerian independent artists spend on a release and promotion, and what are typical price points for packages, courses, mentorship and management?

### Takeaway
Credible hard numbers are almost all from label-level or star-level spending (₦20m-₦500m, $20k-$300k). Genuine indie-tier price data is thin and anecdotal. Emerging artists describe promotion as the most expensive line item and say they reinvest everything they earn from music. Existing "learn to promote" products sold to indies are cheap ($3-$25, mostly unrated), which sets a low price anchor for the ₦35K guide.

### Cited Findings
Label and star-tier benchmarks (not indie, but they set the "what it takes" narrative):
- Don Jazzy (Mavin Records) said $150,000-$300,000 is needed to service a new artist from debut single to first project, and up to ₦500m "from relative obscurity" to a debut project (Pulse paraphrase, Sept 2025) — [Pulse, 29 Sep 2025](https://www.pulse.ng/story/naira500m-to-break-an-artist-why-is-it-so-expensive-to-market-music-in-nigeria-2025092909480636485)
- The same article says the high cost "generally favours the bigger mainstream artists with the deep pockets to service all platforms at scale," and that "All is not lost for emerging and independent artists who can deploy more cost-effective marketing models" (article text) — same source
- A video headline on Pulse puts the average cost to blow up an Afrobeats song at ₦50m; the full remarks were not visible, so treat this as a headline claim only — same source
- Pulse explainer, July 2024. It cites March 2023 figures (USD at 2023 rates):
  - Mid-level producers: $2,000-$5,000 per track.
  - A-list producers: up to $15,000-$20,000 per track.
  - Mixing to streaming standard: up to $2,000.
  - Top video director: up to $50,000.
  - Social ads for one flagship song: at least $20,000.
  - Average cost to service one song: $45,000.
  - Instagram influencers: up to ₦5m per campaign.
  - TikTok and Snapchat influencers: upwards of ₦3m.
  - Twitter influencers: up to ₦200,000 per tweet.
  - Radio: "millions of naira" for up to 100 plays a week.
  - Source: [Pulse Explainer](https://www.pulse.ng/story/pulse-explainer-why-is-music-marketing-expensive-in-nigeria-2024072609264922855)
- Iyanya: "The cheapest I have spent on a song since January 2021 is 27m." Also: "When I dropped 'LIKE' FT Davido and kizz Daniel, we spent 52million, song, Video and promotion." (2023, naira) — same Pulse explainer
- Music videos "now exceed 20 million naira" (2025) — [Pulse, 27 Aug 2025](https://www.pulse.ng/story/the-state-of-music-marketing-in-nigeria-so-expensive-yet-little-return-2025082718333654913)
- Bella Shmurda said he spent $40,000 on promotion for a song that did not succeed: "The song didn't go well, even after 40K USD spent on promotion." The article gives no date; the URL slug suggests June 2025 — [Pulse](https://www.pulse.ng/story/bella-shmurda-narrates-how-he-spent-dollar40k-on-a-song-that-still-failed-to-blow-up-2025060619455630648)

Emerging-artist anecdotes (self-reported, Zikoko, 18 May 2025, small sample of 5, not representative):
- BDMNZZ: "Promotion is the most expensive part of the music grind." He earns about $300 a month from clothing design and reinvests it in music.
- Tsuni: studio time about ₦15,000/hour. Producers and engineers each about ₦100,000 per song.
- Dela: "Studio sessions, visuals, and promos cost a lot, so I pour everything I earn from music back into it." She also said "I still feel broke most of the time."
- Boy Nxxt Door: "Some months I earn ₦150k, other times it's 10x that."
- Muyii receives a ₦77,000 monthly NYSC stipend.
- Source: [Zikoko](https://www.zikoko.com/?p=347627)

Payola and blog costs:
- Tola Sarumi tweeted (unverified allegation) that Daddy Freeze "was charging mandem over 100k to play their music." The article implies naira. Daddy Freeze said "An artist has no problem paying Instablog or Linda Ikeji's Blog or Tunde Ednut or any other blogger" — [Pulse, Aug 2024](https://www.pulse.ng/story/watch-daddy-freeze-and-eldee-the-don-speak-on-payola-in-nigerian-music-and-on-nigeria-2024081121151449834)
- A Pulse piece frames payola as a dilemma for non-A-list artists, with exposure seen as essential. It gives no price list — [Pulse, Aug 2024](https://www.pulse.ng/story/pulse-blogger-payola-a-necessity-or-luxury-2024080205152652100)
- Older context: marketer Emeka Kalu said "It has become cheap to produce 10 to 12 songs than to promote just one song" (Dec 2015, no figures) — [National Daily](https://nationaldailyng.com/marketer-laments-high-cost-of-promoting-music-of-budding-talents/)

Education and promo product price points found (listing claims, unverified; all show 0 ratings and no visible sales count):
- Selar, "Music Promotion Nigerian Masterclass / 2026 Music Promotion Playbook": $3.00. It claims "talent is only 20% of the equation" — [Selar](https://selar.com/39re7q2q13)
- Selar, "The Art of Becoming the Next Big Thing" ebook: $5.25 — [Selar](https://selar.com/35l413)
- Selar, "Musician Money Guide 2021": $7.53 shown against $33.87 — [Selar](https://selar.com/upj7)
- Selar, "Spotify Streaming Bundle" (3 courses, De Brainiac Digital): listed at $25, from $75. The sales copy says ₦25,000 launch price and about ₦150,000 value. It offers a 100% money-back guarantee if there is no "noticeable increase" in streams, which is an unverified claim — [Selar](https://selar.com/explode-your-streams)
- AltSchool Africa, Music Marketing and Promotion course: $20, with an ASIC-accredited certificate (accreditation unverified on the page) — [AltSchool](https://altschoolafrica.com/programs/music-marketing-promotion)
- Berklee in Nigeria / Tiwa Savage Intensive, Lagos, 23-26 Apr 2026: no tuition, but participants pay travel and housing — [Berklee/TurnTable](https://www.turntablecharts.com/news/2079)

### Inferences
- The ₦35K guide (about $26) sits above the $3-$25 DIY-ebook tier and below any live-service price. ₦99,700 (about $74) is cheap against label spend but expensive against the earnings anecdotes above (₦150k-₦300k monthly incomes).
- A mid-tier indie "wasted spend" figure of ₦100k-₦1m per release is plausible from the anecdotes (₦100k per producer per song, ₦15k/hr studio, 3m naira-scale influencer fees), but I found no survey that states it. Do not present it as a measured figure.
- The big-money stories (Don Jazzy, Bella Shmurda, Iyanya) normalise the belief that promotion needs six- or seven-figure budgets. That feeds the "money beats strategy" objection.

### Gaps
- No published indie-tier price lists for Nigerian blog, DJ or radio payola, IG promo pages, mixing and mastering, cover art, or management commission. No Reddit, Nairaland or X threads were retrievable.
- Nigerian playlist-pitching prices were not found; only Fiverr and Upwork global listings appeared in search results.
- No Nigerian music-management retainer or percentage data found.
- No price found for a human-led mentorship or paid cohort for Nigerian indies.

## 2. What have artists already tried, and how did it go?

### Takeaway
Artists keep paying for influencers, blogs, ads and stream boosts, and commentators report poor returns and eroding trust. Fake streams are widely suspected on Nigerian charts and are being penalised. The strongest "failed after paying" story is from a star (Bella Shmurda's $40k), but industry voices say the same logic hurts indies more.

### Cited Findings
- The Pulse headline reads "So expensive, yet little return." The body says "results rarely match the investment" and "Audiences are showing apathy." Dunsin Bankole: "The audience can now tell when an influencer is paid to post, and it's affecting how marketing campaigns convert." — [Pulse, 27 Aug 2025](https://www.pulse.ng/story/the-state-of-music-marketing-in-nigeria-so-expensive-yet-little-return-2025082718333654913)
- The same article quotes a tweet about "songs that people can't escape on TikTok yet won't even bother to press play on streaming platforms." This indicates viral exposure that does not convert to streams — same source
- Olamide Ademeso: "Artists are focused on numbers and engagement-based marketing rather than on storytelling" — same source
- Honour Aghedo: "The cost of breaking out is more expensive than ever" — same source
- Bella Shmurda's $40k flop (see Q1)
- Stream farming: BNXN said "There are streaming farms in Nigeria now" (2022). Joey Akan: "Everyone goes to the farmland." — [BusinessDay, 26 Nov 2022](https://businessday.ng/arts-and-life/article/explainer-streaming-farms-black-market-of-music-business/)
- Pulse (Aug 2024; modified Dec 2025) says stream manipulation "appears to still be endemic in Nigeria" on Apple Music. It says manipulation is capital-intensive, "mostly exclusive to bigger artists and labels," and that smaller artists are mostly the ones being targeted by crackdowns — [Pulse](https://www.pulse.ng/story/are-streaming-platforms-doing-enough-to-curb-stream-farming-2024092616090526202)
- Blaqbonez, 30 Jul 2025 (X): "For now, let's keep acting like the charts mean anything more than a showcase of the people who can afford to buy it." Magixx: "Y'all faking your numbers." — [NotJustOk, 31 Jul 2025](https://notjustok.com/news/magixx-and-blaqbonez-slam-fake-streaming-numbers-in-nigerian-music-industry/)
- Spotify's Bryan Johnson (Spotify for Artists Masterclass, reported 4 Jun 2025): services charging artists for playlist placement are scams. "You can't buy your way onto a playlist." — [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2025/06/04/spotify-cracks-down-on-fake-streams-warns-artists-globally/)
- A promotional post for MuzikHub (labelled "Ad," 28 Apr 2026) claims independent Nigerian artists hit a wall at 1,000 streams. It claims they skip Spotify for Artists, release with no plan, and are "completely blind" on listener data. This is a vendor's claim, not research — [MuzikHub](https://muzikhub.vercel.app/blog/how-to-get-your-first-1000-streams-on-spotify-guide-for-nigerian-artists)

### Inferences
- The recurring failure pattern is: pay for reach (influencers, blogs, ads, boosted streams) without a conversion path to saves, followers and repeat listeners. That fits the "strategy before spend" positioning directly.
- Few artists publicly name regrets about buying streams, because it is stigmatised. Expect under-reporting.

### Gaps
- No first-person indie regret accounts on Reddit, Nairaland or X were retrievable. No survey of what indies tried and how it went.
- No verifiable testimonials from any Nigerian growth-education provider.

## 3. Buying triggers and urgency signals

### Takeaway
Direct evidence on triggers is thin. The inferable triggers are competition and saturation, the cost of promotion eating the budget, and visible peer success. I found no quantified "amount lost before seeking help."

### Cited Findings
- Dunsin Bankole: "Consumers are spoilt for choice. There's just too much music" — [Pulse, 27 Aug 2025](https://www.pulse.ng/story/the-state-of-music-marketing-in-nigeria-so-expensive-yet-little-return-2025082718333654913)
- A media personality said "Compared to even 10 years ago, more artists are now vying for similar positions" — [Pulse Explainer](https://www.pulse.ng/story/pulse-explainer-why-is-music-marketing-expensive-in-nigeria-2024072609264922855)
- Dela and BDMNZZ say they pour all music income back into studio and promotion, so each release is a cash-constrained bet — [Zikoko, 18 May 2025](https://www.zikoko.com/?p=347627)
- Spotify stream deductions (Feb-Apr 2025) hit several Nigerian artists' songs, creating a fear trigger. Spotify has said nothing specific on this wave — [Pulse, 4 Apr 2025](https://www.pulse.ng/story/spotify-continues-wide-spread-stream-deduction-2025040412102939428)
- Streams of Nigerian independent artists rose 75% year on year on Spotify in 2025. That is visible peer momentum — [Music In Africa](https://www.musicinafrica.net/node/385257)

### Inferences
- The best-supported triggers are (a) a flop after a paid push, (b) cash running out after a release, (c) seeing peers or the Daily Top 50 move, (d) an upcoming release.
- Deadlines (release dates) are plausible but not documented in any source I found.
- I found no evidence on how much artists lose before seeking help. Use the anecdotes in Q1 only as illustrations.

### Gaps
- No survey or interview data on purchase triggers. No data on time-to-purchase or amount lost.

## 4. Barriers and objections

### Takeaway
The documented barriers are cost of living and low income, widespread scepticism of promotion and of chart figures, and the "pay to play" belief. I found no source that quantifies refusal to buy education.

### Cited Findings
- Artist incomes are small and irregular. Muyii's NYSC stipend is ₦77,000 a month. Boy Nxxt Door earns ₦150k in some months. Dela's media job pays about ₦300,000 a month — [Zikoko, 18 May 2025](https://www.zikoko.com/?p=347627)
- Spotify Premium costs ₦1,600/month (about $1.17) in Nigeria, and 1m streams are worth about $300 in Nigeria against up to $10,000 in Sweden. Low per-stream value caps earnings from DSPs — [TechCabal, 16 Mar 2026](https://techcabal.com/2026/03/16/spotify-payouts-show-nigerian-artists-earned-about-₦2-per-stream-in-2025/)
- Pay-to-play beliefs: Blaqbonez says charts show "the people who can afford to buy it"; Pulse says marketing has become "a matter of who could spend the most money to create the biggest buzz" — [NotJustOk](https://notjustok.com/news/magixx-and-blaqbonez-slam-fake-streaming-numbers-in-nigerian-music-industry/), [Pulse](https://www.pulse.ng/story/the-state-of-music-marketing-in-nigeria-so-expensive-yet-little-return-2025082718333654913)
- David Adeyemi (PR executive): "The Nigerian music industry is a complex one-room apartment" — same Pulse source
- Scepticism of guarantees: Spotify itself says "Paid 3rd-party services that guarantee streams aren't legitimate" — [Spotify Support](https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/third-party-services-that-guarantee-streams/)
- Competing cheap products offer guarantees (e.g., a "100% Money-Back Guarantee" on a $25 bundle). That makes guarantees look commonplace and weak — [Selar](https://selar.com/explode-your-streams)
- Naira context: the official rate was about ₦1,351/$ in Nov 2026, about 6.3% stronger than Nov 2025, with a parallel rate of ₦1,430-1,440 — [Within Nigeria](https://www.withinnigeria.com/2026/11/12/dollar-to-naira-today-november-12-2026-nfem-and-cbn-rate-near-%E2%82%A61350-as-black-market-hits-%E2%82%A61430/?amp=1)

### Inferences
- Expect objections such as "I can't afford it," "it only works for people with connections or money," "another guru," and "will you guarantee streams." The sources support each theme but none measure how common it is.
- The $3-$25 price anchor on Selar means ₦35K must prove clear added value over the cheap tier.

### Gaps
- No trust, scam-prevalence or willingness-to-pay survey for Nigerian music education. No data on the share of artists who can afford ₦35k-₦100k.

## 5. Macro data

### Takeaway
Nigerian streaming is growing quickly in total, but the market is flooded. Globally, 88% of tracks got 1,000 streams or fewer in 2025. Per-stream payout in Nigeria is about ₦2. Platforms are actively removing artificial streams, which directly hurts anyone who bought them.

### Cited Findings
Spotify Nigeria 2025 (Loud & Clear, reported 16 Mar 2026):
- Nigerian artists generated over ₦60bn (about $43.8-43.9m) in Spotify revenue in 2025, up more than 140% over two years. 2024 was ₦58bn ($42.45m) — [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/03/16/spotify-payouts-show-nigerian-artists-earned-about-₦2-per-stream-in-2025/)
- 30.3bn streams, 1.6bn listening hours, and an implied rate of about ₦1.98 per stream (the article's own calculation, not a Spotify-published rate) — same source
- About 58% of royalties went to independent artists or labels — [Music In Africa](https://www.musicinafrica.net/node/385257)
- Independent artists' streams rose 75% year on year. Local consumption in Nigeria rose 170% year on year. Nigerian artists made up over 80% of the Daily Top 50, with nearly 2,000 added to editorial playlists — same source
- Interspace (a distributor, so promotional) says earnings grew only 3.45% from 2024 to 2025, so the 140% growth was 2023-2025. It says Audiomack has 15.3m monthly active users and 4.9m daily users in Nigeria (citing Music Ally, Dec 2024), and is the number one music app on iOS and Android there. It cites IFPI 2026: Sub-Saharan Africa recorded music revenue rose 15.2% to $120m, with South Africa at 78.1% and Nigeria within the remaining 21.9% (about $26m). Published 21 Jul 2026 — [Interspace Music](https://interspacemusic.com/blog/nigerian-artists-spotify-earnings-grew-3-45-last-year-its-220-million-listeners-are-on-audiomack/)
- Global saturation, Luminate 2025 Year-End: 106,000 tracks delivered per day (up 7% from 99,000 in 2024), 253m audio ISRCs, 88% of tracks with 1,000 streams or fewer, and about 120.5m tracks with 0-10 streams — [Hypebot, 20 Jan 2026](https://www.hypebot.com/luminate-reports-music-streaming-saturation-2025-2026/). These are global, not Nigeria-specific.
- Spotify policy: Spotify withholds royalties on confirmed artificial streaming, corrects stream counts, may remove tracks from playlists, and charges labels and distributors per track for flagrant cases. Distributors may warn or suspend accounts — [Spotify Support](https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/third-party-services-that-guarantee-streams/)
- Stream deductions hit Nigerian artists from Feb to Apr 2025. Spotify gave no explanation — [Pulse, 4 Apr 2025](https://www.pulse.ng/story/spotify-continues-wide-spread-stream-deduction-2025040412102939428)
- Apple Music says manipulation is below 1% of monthly streams globally, deducts manipulated streams in near real time, and may blacklist the artist and distributor — [Pulse, Aug 2024](https://www.pulse.ng/story/are-streaming-platforms-doing-enough-to-curb-stream-farming-2024092616090526202)
- Bots can stream a song up to 1,000 times per minute (BusinessDay's description of the tactic) — [BusinessDay, 2022](https://businessday.ng/arts-and-life/article/explainer-streaming-farms-black-market-of-music-business/)

### Inferences
- With about ₦2 per stream, 100,000 streams is only about ₦200k in Spotify royalties (my calculation from the implied rate; it also assumes the artist has no split). Chasing the "100K streams" milestone is a credibility and career milestone, not an income milestone. Pitch it that way.
- No data splits the ₦60bn between a few stars and the long tail. Do not claim the typical indie earns a given amount.
- Because Nigeria-specific tracks-uploaded and low-stream shares were not found, use the Luminate global figures and label them as global.

### Gaps
- No Nigeria-specific count of artists, tracks uploaded, or share of tracks under a stream threshold.
- No Spotify, Boomplay, Apple Music or YouTube Nigeria user counts beyond Audiomack (and that is Dec 2024, via a distributor blog).
- No Audiomack or Boomplay payout rates found. Boomplay royalty issues appeared in a search result title (a Medium post claiming Sony, The Orchard, Merlin and AWAL exited over unpaid royalties) that I could not verify.
- No Nigeria-specific data on artists who lost streams after buying bots. Chart Data and The Debut Hub commentary on the Feb-Apr 2025 deductions is secondhand.

## 6. Competitive and peer landscape

### Takeaway
What I could verify is a long tail of cheap Selar ebooks and one scalable paid course (AltSchool), all with unverified claims, and no visible reviews. Free tools such as Spotify's own materials and grant bootcamps exist. I found no established Nigerian "strategy before spend" brand. That absence may reflect search limits, not a real market gap.

### Cited Findings
- Selar listings (see Q1 for prices), all showing 0 ratings. Claims include "proven and guaranteed program," "talent is only 20% of the equation," and "without bots, without fake playlists" — [Selar](https://selar.com/upj7), [Selar](https://selar.com/39re7q2q13), [Selar](https://selar.com/explode-your-streams)
- AltSchool Africa, $20 course: a "strategic blueprint" with modules on digital readiness, the Afrobeats industry, and music marketing. Positioning is a certificate for artists and music professionals — [AltSchool](https://altschoolafrica.com/programs/music-marketing-promotion)
- Free and grant options: Berklee in Nigeria / Tiwa Savage Intensive, tuition-free, 23-26 Apr 2026, applications 24 Feb-20 Mar 2026 — [TurnTable](https://www.turntablecharts.com/news/2079). Other bootcamps appeared in search but I did not open them: Création Africa Fund (French Embassy, CcHUB), Amplify Bootcamp, Showbiz101 2026 (Lagos).
- Distributors double as educators. MuzikHub and InterSpace publish how-to content that funnels artists to their products (both promotional) — [MuzikHub](https://muzikhub.vercel.app/blog/how-to-get-your-first-1000-streams-on-spotify-guide-for-nigerian-artists), [Interspace](https://interspacemusic.com/blog/nigerian-artists-spotify-earnings-grew-3-45-last-year-its-220-million-listeners-are-on-audiomack/)
- Spotify for Artists runs free masterclasses and says playlist-placement sellers are scams — [Nairametrics, 4 Jun 2025](https://nairametrics.com/2025/06/04/spotify-cracks-down-on-fake-streams-warns-artists-globally/)

### Inferences
- Gap candidates from what I saw: (a) "how to promote" content stresses tactics and virality, with few sellers explaining how to stop wasting money; (b) guarantees are common and not credible, so a no-guarantee, diagnosis-first stance could differentiate; (c) no visible social proof anywhere in the cheap tier. Treat these as hypotheses.
- Free supply (Spotify masterclasses, grants) caps what a pure-information product can charge. The ₦99,700 Intensive needs a feedback or implementation component to justify its price.

### Gaps
- No named Nigerian competitor with a ₦50k-₦500k cohort or coaching price. No management or PR agency rate cards. No verifiable testimonials. Instagram and DM-sold services are not searchable.

## 7. Pain ranking (my scoring)

Scale: 1-5 on each of four criteria (frequency of mention in sources, money lost, emotional intensity, whether people already pay to fix it). The total is out of 20. The scores are my judgement from the cited evidence, not measured survey data.

| Rank | Pain | Freq | Money | Emotion | Already pays | Total | Justification |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Promotion costs too much for too little return | 5 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 18 | The most repeated theme: Pulse (Aug 2025), Zikoko, Don Jazzy, Bella Shmurda $40k flop. People already pay influencers, blogs and ads. |
| 2 | Cannot tell what works (influencer, blog or ad spend does not convert to streams) | 4 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 15 | Pulse: "audience can now tell when an influencer is paid to post." The viral-but-not-streaming tweet. Inferred from industry voices, not artist surveys. |
| 3 | Invisible in saturated supply (too much music) | 4 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 14 | Luminate: 88% of tracks at 1,000 streams or fewer (global). Bankole: "too much music." |
| 4 | Feels pay-to-play, with connections and money needed (distrust) | 4 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 14 | Blaqbonez and Magixx on fake numbers, Daddy Freeze payola exchange. Tied to the trust barrier in Q4. |
| 5 | Low and irregular income, so every release is a cash gamble | 3 | 4 | 4 | 2 | 13 | Zikoko anecdotes, ₦2 per stream. n=5 and the sample is self-selected. |
| 6 | Risk of penalties from bought or fake streams | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 12 | Feb-Apr 2025 Spotify deductions; Spotify penalty policy. Few artists admit to buying. |
| 7 | Lack of release strategy or Spotify-for-Artists basics | 2 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 9 | Only from vendor content (MuzikHub). Weak evidence. The cheap ebook tier shows some paying, but at $3-$25. |

### Inferences
- Urgency is moderate-to-high, but the evidence is about spending and frustration, not about purchase of education. The sources show artists will pay for promotion, so the offer must be framed as reducing that spend, not as another expense.
- Willingness to pay ranges. The cheap tier shows $3-$25 is normal for guides. There is no verified evidence that ₦99,700 is a normal price point for indies. It needs a proof or feedback mechanism.

### Gaps
- Scores rest on press coverage and a handful of anecdotes. A survey, or a scrape of Reddit, Nairaland and X threads, would be needed to firm these up. That scraping was not possible in this session.
