# Pains and Frustrations of Independent Music Artists (Nigeria first; Africa and global labelled) — as of Nov 2026

Method note: ~25 searches/fetches. Reddit, Nairaland, X, Facebook and YouTube comments did NOT surface in search results and could not be fetched directly, so first-person artist voice below comes mainly from Nigerian journalism features (Zikoko, BellaNaija, Legit, Pulse), the Spotify Community forum, and Substack/Hypebot essays. Quotes in "double quotes" are verbatim as returned by the fetch tool (fetch tool summarises pages, so treat exact wording as "as extracted"; re-verify before using in public copy). Anything labelled PARAPHRASE is not a quote. Dates given where known.

---

## 1. What artists complain about after releasing (low streams, no reach, invisibility, flops)

### Takeaway
The dominant post-release pain is that releasing is easy but being heard is expensive and unpredictable; even industry insiders say doing everything right no longer guarantees a break. Invisibility, "standing still while others move", and the sense that nobody is coming to help recur across Nigerian first-person accounts.

### Cited Findings
- Industry exec, Nigeria (Aug 2025): "The cost of breaking out is more expensive than ever. You can do everything right, and the artist won't blow up." — Honour Aghedo, Music and Marketing Executive, Pulse Nigeria. [Source](https://www.pulse.ng/story/the-state-of-music-marketing-in-nigeria-so-expensive-yet-little-return-2025082718333654913)
- Same article (paraphrase): too many artists chase the same listeners; the market is not growing fast enough to absorb supply; audiences can tell paid influencer posts, hurting conversion. [Source](https://www.pulse.ng/story/the-state-of-music-marketing-in-nigeria-so-expensive-yet-little-return-2025082718333654913)
- Upcoming Nigerian artist Reehaa (BellaNaija, 19 May 2026): "Social media makes it very easy to feel like everybody else is moving forward while you are standing still." / "There were so many moments I genuinely considered quitting music entirely." / "At some point, I stopped believing in myself completely." / "I also had this mindset that Instagram had to be perfect and polished before I could show up there." [Source](https://www.bellanaija.com/2026/05/reehaa-on-being-an-upcoming-artist-in-todays-nigerian-music-industry/)
- Zikoko first-person (3 Jul 2025), Owerri artist who moved to Lagos: title "I Moved to Lagos to Become a Musician. I Ended Up Broke and Invisible"; "Having this big dream and realising nobody is coming — it's just you and you alone — can be hard."; "I felt sad and was sinking into depression."; "I went back to Owerri with no music and no record deal." Timeline: first song Dec 2016, Lagos 2021, back home after. [Source](https://www.zikoko.com/?p=351770)
- Nigerian market is flooded: Mdundo's West Africa licensing lead says 100+ tracks are released weekly in Nigeria (insider claim, platform has a commercial interest). [Source](https://www.hypebot.com/stream-farms-and-the-west-african-music-market-an-insiders-view.md)
- Global (labelled GLOBAL): Ditto survey of 5,039 indie artists in 112 countries (South Africa 3rd largest cohort with 929 respondents): 43% say money is the biggest barrier to growth; 43% "don't know how to pitch for editorial playlists"; only 1% say editorial playlists are their biggest source of new fans; 67% say social video is their biggest source of new fans. [Source](https://dittomusic.com/en/independent-music-report-2026) (Caveat: respondents all distribute via Ditto, so skew to active releasers; no Nigeria cut published.)
- GLOBAL essay, 9 May 2026: "The algorithm doesn't reward what's "good" it rewards what's most compulsively watchable." / "I'm thoroughly in the ranks of having given up being heard widely but still making a lot of music." / "...they wouldn't see our posts unless we paid to "boost" them." — Battery Operated Orchestra (Substack). [Source](https://batteryoperatedorchestra.substack.com/p/slave-to-the-algorithm-why-working)

### Inferences
- The "nobody is coming" / "standing still" framing maps directly onto the target segment (already released, little traction). Language like "I did everything" and "invisible" is validated by multiple sources.
- Frequency: high (appears in nearly every first-person source found). Severity signals: considering quitting (Reehaa), depression and returning home (Zikoko), selling a phone (see section 2).

### Gaps
- No Nairaland/Reddit/X posts retrieved, so no raw "my song got 200 streams" posts with counts. Recommend a manual pass on r/NigerianMusic, Nairaland Music section, and X search ("nobody is listening" + Nigeria).

## 2. Promotion mistakes, scams, and money lost

### Takeaway
Nigerian artists face a pay-to-be-seen market (influencers, blogs, radio, "marketers") with rising rates and falling trust, plus fake-stream/playlist schemes that can get releases deleted. Reported individual losses are mostly given as "emptied my account" rather than numbers; hard naira figures exist mainly for label-scale spending and influencer rate cards.

### Cited Findings
**Pay-to-play marketing and rising costs**
- Don Jazzy (Mavin): about $150,000 to $300,000 to service a new artist from debut single to first project, and "up to ₦500 million can be expended" to take an artist from obscurity to debut project (as reported by Pulse, 29 Sep 2025; label-scale, not indie scale). [Source](https://www.pulse.ng/story/naira500m-to-break-an-artist-why-is-it-so-expensive-to-market-music-in-nigeria-2025092909480636485)
- David Adeyemi (Marketing Exec): "Even Influencers now want to be paid 'Afrobeats To The World' because there's more money in the ecosystem." Article states costs favour bigger mainstream artists. [Source](https://www.pulse.ng/story/naira500m-to-break-an-artist-why-is-it-so-expensive-to-market-music-in-nigeria-2025092909480636485)
- Dunsin Bankole (We Talk Sound): "The audience can now tell when an influencer is paid to post, and it's affecting how marketing campaigns convert." [Source](https://www.pulse.ng/story/the-state-of-music-marketing-in-nigeria-so-expensive-yet-little-return-2025082718333654913)
- Influencer rate indications (OLDER, 29 Mar 2023): Instagram up to ₦5 million per campaign; TikTok/Snapchat upwards of ₦3 million; Twitter up to ₦200,000 per tweet; major radio "millions" for up to 100 plays a week. Iyanya (X, 27 Mar 2023): "Promoting music has become very expensive, if you are lucky to get a song go viral make you Thank God." Joey Akan (X, 28 Mar 2023): "Tiktok isn't the last frontier. It's already cash-captured." [Source](https://www.pulse.ng/story/pulse-explainer-why-is-music-marketing-expensive-in-nigeria-2024072609264922855) (URL slug says 2024 but fetched article dated 29 March 2023.)
- Same article, article headline of 2025 piece cites Pulse video claiming "an average of N50 million to blow up an Afrobeats song now" (sidebar, unverified). [Source](https://www.pulse.ng/story/the-state-of-music-marketing-in-nigeria-so-expensive-yet-little-return-2025082718333654913)
- Individual indie loss: Zikoko artist (Jul 2025) paying a radio-airplay "marketer" in 2020: "I emptied my account and sold my phone to pay the marketer." Amount not stated. [Source](https://www.zikoko.com/?p=351770)
- Funding pain (OLDER, 24 Sep 2022, Legit): Black Votary: "I have an EP coming that I've spent close to a million on the recording of the songs" and "I can save for as long as six months just to get money to do a couple of songs." / "I need to get funds to promote the project." Gida Hills: "I eventually had to borrow money because I had to pay people." Leemzy: "The director billed me an amount that I personally couldn't fend for." [Source](https://www.legit.ng/entertainment/celebrities/1493970-i-save-long-6-months-3-upcoming-artistes-struggles-funding-music-careers)
- Agent scams (OLDER, 2017): upcoming artists reportedly paid agents about ₦50,000 for platform placement and the agents disappeared (anonymous allegation). [Source](https://www.pulse.ng/story/pulse-exclusive-how-nigerian-artists-are-underpaid-by-local-digital-distribution-2024080104292887615)
- Ghana (GHANA label, 11 Jul 2025): artist Moliy: "being independent or working in Ghana, or I guess anywhere else, if you want to collaborate or be seen, most of it is payola". [Source](https://mobile.ghanaweb.com/GhanaHomePage/entertainment/If-you-want-to-be-seen-in-Ghana-you-re-often-required-to-pay-payola-Moliy-on-music-journey-1991293)

**Producer/beat hiring scams (Nigeria, May 2026)**
- Pulse Mix featured post (likely sponsored by Graviitalbeats; treat as promotional): artists paid ₦30,000 to ₦150,000+ for beats that never arrived; "There is no refund mechanism."; "Ghost producers do not just take money. They take timing." Anonymous, unverified. [Source](https://www.pulse.ng/story/nigerian-artists-and-producers-are-both-getting-burned-graviitalbeats-is-the-saviour-2026051612234604895)

**Stream farms and fake streams**
- Zikoko (25 Nov 2022): "streaming farms are companies designed to add fake streams to a song using bots or multiple phones at a time"; "These bots can stream music as many as 1000 times in just one minute."; using them "still counts as a direct violation of the terms and services of streaming platforms"; no explicit Nigerian law against them. Chart rivalry accusations (Ruger/BNXN) referenced. [Source](https://www.zikoko.com/?p=289991)
- Mdundo insider (Hypebot, date not captured): stream farming "does nothing to actually build a career." / "eventually it will catch up and potentially ruin a career." Claims chart positions across Nigeria are influenced by it. [Source](https://www.hypebot.com/stream-farms-and-the-west-african-music-market-an-insiders-view.md)
- Spotify cracked down on fake streams and warned artists globally (Nairametrics, 4 Jun 2025) — headline only, not fetched. [Source](https://nairametrics.com/2025/06/04/spotify-cracks-down-on-fake-streams-warns-artists-globally/)

**Playlist scams and takedowns (GLOBAL, Spotify Community)**
- Thread (Spotify Community; original post screenshots dated Jan 2024, replies undated): ImNewman (CØVES): "We have never paid for any music promotion service." yet "we have had a release taken down from Spotify for suspicion of artificial streams." KobeBanks: "I did it for $100/release." (Spotify's own Marquee) then "last night Spotify took down my entire album." CrystolbolRecords: "we spent thousands of dollars to release the song" then removed for "fake bot". Krupted: "15 albums pulled offline". [Source](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Other-Podcasts-Partners-etc/Spotify-removed-our-release-for-bot-streams-even-after-i/m-p/6622850/highlight/true)
- Playlist scam pattern (22 Mar 2024, Two Story Melody): "Scam companies add music from small indie artists to their own big playlists." / "The company says they can re-add the artist to the playlist – for a fee." / "In my experience, every single company that uses this approach has built their playlists with bots." [Source](https://twostorymelody.com/a-spotify-scam/)
- Spotify Community "Stop Playlist Scams" idea thread exists with 20+ pages (not fetched). [Source](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Live-Ideas/Stop-Playlist-Scams/idi-p/5907595/page/8)

**Undisclosed paid influencer promos (GLOBAL/US, NPR, Jul 2026)**
- US TikTok influencers charge about $150 to $600 per song video; anonymous influencer: "it's sort of understood that you don't ever put #ad"; "It's sort of understood that if you do disclose it, it'll hurt the performance of the content." James Blake: "there's not a single part of the system that isn't faked". [Source](https://www.npr.org/nx-s1-5849926)

### Inferences
- The most commonly reported mechanism is "paid, got nothing visible, can't prove why". Severity examples with money: phone sold, savings emptied, borrowing, saving 6 months per couple of songs.
- Distinct audience-trust point: Nigerian execs themselves say paid influencer posts are now recognised as paid and convert worse. This supports a "stop wasting money" angle.
- Playlist-takedown fear is global and often affects people who say they paid nothing; the Nigerian relevance is inferred (Spotify is a main target for Nigerian artists) but not directly evidenced in my sources.

### Gaps
- No verbatim Nigerian artist quote with a specific naira amount lost to Instagram promo pages, playlist pitching, Meta ads, Audiomack/Boomplay promo packages. Searches returned only label-scale figures. Meta-ads waste for Nigerian musicians: found nothing specific.
- Boomplay/Audiomack paid "promo packages": no source found.

## 3. Structural pains (strategy, budget, team, platform confusion, payouts, distributors, currency)

### Takeaway
Structural pain clusters around (a) tiny local payouts so streams don't fund promotion, (b) opaque or unreliable distributors/managers/CMOs, (c) no budget/team, and (d) platform confusion (Audiomack for discovery vs Spotify for royalties).

### Cited Findings
**Payouts and currency**
- Nigerian artists earned ₦60 billion on 30.3 billion Spotify streams in 2025, about ₦1.98 per stream; 1 million streams in Nigeria generate about $300 vs up to $10,000 in Sweden; premium plan about ₦1,600 ($1.17) in Nigeria. Earnings grew only 3.45% 2024-2025 despite local consumption up 170%. About 58% of Nigerian Spotify royalties went to independents and labels; Empire, Sony, UMG handle 68% of volume. (TechCabal, 23 Mar 2026) [Source](https://techcabal.com/2026/03/23/local-streams-are-reducing-spotify-payouts-for-nigerian-artists/)
- Pulse (undated, 2026): 1M streams from Nigeria earn "an equivalent of $300", per Sarz; Nigerian subscription raised from ₦1,000 to ₦1,600 but inflation and weaker naira stop payouts rising; Burna Boy (2025) said topping Nigerian charts "isn't worth celebrating" (paraphrase by article). [Source](https://www.pulse.ng/story/dollar300-for-1m-streams-why-is-nigerian-streaming-payout-low-2026022613451615974)
- OkayAfrica (16 Apr 2026): Nigeria's real streaming value fell nearly 50%; "The money exists. It just has nowhere to go."; incomplete metadata registration stops international earnings; Kenya's MCSK denied licence 2025-26; Nigeria introduced Collective Management Regulations in 2025. [Source](https://www.okayafrica.com/global-sound-local-loss-africas-music-money-gap/1427679)
- Spotify 1,000-stream minimum before a track earns royalties (introduced 2024); Kenyan coverage and RouteNote analysis exist (not fetched; headlines only). [Source](https://nairobinews.nation.africa/spotify-new-rules-to-affect-kenyan-artistes-royalties-earnings/), [Source](https://routenote.com/blog/spotifys-1000-stream-threshold-cost-indie-artists-50-million-in-2024/)

**Distributors, managers, lawyers**
- Zikoko (19 Jul 2025), anonymised artist "Tayo*", unnamed popular Nigerian distributor: 70/30 split, $100 withdrawal threshold ("hundreds of thousands of streams"), over 200,000 mostly foreign Spotify streams showed only $60 in report; "They were taking 30% of the revenue, yet offering zero transparency." / "It was beyond frustrating." [Source](https://www.zikoko.com/?p=353287)
- Zikoko (25 Aug 2025), 10 musicians on management: Papi Gunzo "There was a period where he held my music for over two and a half years."; SosaTTW "A lawyer is supposed to provide clarity. He gave me confusion."; Produca Wa "He won't even respond to our messages, let alone send us a report on how the song is doing."; Samvsthekids "It took constant back-and-forth and borderline begging to get a semblance of a royalty report."; Droxx "I had to resort to publicly calling them out on social media." [Source](https://www.zikoko.com/pop/musicians-realised-management-didnt-have-their-back/)
- OLDER (19 Sep 2017) Pulse on local digital distribution: anonymous label exec: "The network and platform make the most money, the artist only gets peanuts." [Source](https://www.pulse.ng/story/pulse-exclusive-how-nigerian-artists-are-underpaid-by-local-digital-distribution-2024080104292887615)

**Platform confusion (Audiomack vs Spotify)**
- InterSpace Distribution blog (15 Jul 2026; commercial source selling Audiomack/Boomplay delivery, figures unverified): "Audiomack is where a Nigerian career catches fire early."; "Spotify is where Nigerian music earns premium-market royalties."; "Boomplay and Audiomack are treated as optional add-ons, delivered late or not at all." [Source](https://interspacemusic.com/blog/nigerian-artists-earned-n60-billion-on-spotify-in-2025-discovery-still-lives-on-audiomack/)
- Boomplay royalty non-payment: Sony, The Orchard, Merlin, AWAL reported exiting over unpaid royalties (Dec 2024; headlines only). [Source](https://completemusicupdate.com/sony-pulls-catalogue-from-boomplay-as-sources-claim-streaming-platform-has-not-paid-royalties-since-april-2023/)

**Budget/team**
- Global (Ditto, 5,039 artists): 74% earned under $1,000 from music in 2025; 64% of self-releasing artists have a job outside music; 43% say money is the biggest barrier. [Source](https://dittomusic.com/en/independent-music-report-2026)
- Older Nigerian reasons musicians quit (Pulse, 21 Jul 2016): money, pressure, competition, team splits: "The music industry in Nigeria is an extremely costly venture, with little or no guarantee for returns." [Source](https://www.pulse.ng/story/state-of-the-music-5-reasons-why-nigerian-musicians-quit-2024080502064861785)
- Vanity over strategy (paraphrase, Pulse Aug 2025): artists prioritise numbers/engagement and aesthetics; quote: "Artists are focused on numbers and engagement-based marketing rather than on storytelling" — Olamide Ademeso. "The infatuation with controversy, social wins, and viral moments still trumps organic" — Marv Tomide. [Source](https://www.pulse.ng/story/the-state-of-music-marketing-in-nigeria-so-expensive-yet-little-return-2025082718333654913)

### Inferences
- "Streams don't pay enough to fund promotion" means Nigerian indies cannot recoup spend, making wasted promotion more painful than for Western indies. Strong angle for "stop wasting money".
- Confusion over Audiomack vs Spotify is evidenced only by a distributor with a stake; no artist quotes on this found.
- Strategy gap is asserted by execs (numbers over storytelling), not by artists themselves.

### Gaps
- No Nigerian-specific data on naira devaluation affecting artists' ability to pay for international ads/cards (Meta/Spotify Marquee/Distrokid fees). Not found.
- No Nigerian artist quotes on Apple Music/Audiomack payout issues.

## 4. Emotional pains

### Takeaway
Discouragement, comparison, social-media pressure and burnout are well evidenced, mainly global survey data plus a few Nigerian first-person accounts.

### Cited Findings
- Nigeria: Reehaa (BellaNaija, 19 May 2026): "At some point, I stopped believing in myself completely." / "A friend once told me that maybe I was not hungry enough for success." / "But loving music and surviving the music industry are two different things." [Source](https://www.bellanaija.com/2026/05/reehaa-on-being-an-upcoming-artist-in-todays-nigerian-music-industry/)
- Nigeria: Zikoko (Jul 2025): "I felt sad and was sinking into depression." [Source](https://www.zikoko.com/?p=351770)
- GLOBAL: Ditto "Creative 2026" (Hypebot, 13 Mar 2026; about 2,000 respondents): 86% report significant mental strain/burnout; social media pressure is top cause (52%), financial insecurity 24%; Lee Parsons: "it has also created an environment where artists feel they can never switch off." [Source](https://www.hypebot.com/86-of-musicians-report-mental-strain-as-creative-burnout-hits-all-time-high)
- GLOBAL: Ditto Independent Music Report 2026 (5,039 artists): 32% considered quitting in past year (43% among US); 75.3% name financial pressure, marketing/self-promotion, or day-job balance as biggest stress. The page's 61% mental-health headline conflicts internally with 44%/14% breakouts; verify before citing. [Source](https://dittomusic.com/en/independent-music-report-2026)
- GLOBAL essay (May 2026): "it doesn't feel good dedicating your precious little free time to feeding a machine." [Source](https://batteryoperatedorchestra.substack.com/p/slave-to-the-algorithm-why-working)

### Inferences
- "Am I wasting my time / should I quit" is supported by survey and first-person data; family pressure was not found in any source.
- Pressure to produce short video content is a distinct pain from "no streams" (burnout from the promotion treadmill).

### Gaps
- Family pressure and "am I wasting my time" in Nigerian artists' own words: not found (would likely be on X/Nairaland/Reddit).
- Ditto burnout survey methodology unspecified; likely skews to Ditto users.

## 5. Views of producers, consultants, gurus, courses

### Takeaway
Distrust of the "advice economy" is documented globally and Nigerian artists report bad experiences with managers, lawyers, distributors and ghost producers; I found no Nigerian-specific source about music-marketing gurus or courses.

### Cited Findings
- GLOBAL, Hypebot, 9 Sep 2026, Charlie Recksieck: "If you can make a living teaching musicians how to succeed, you don't necessarily need the musicians themselves to succeed." / "The people selling advice often have a much more predictable business model than the people buying it." / "In this scenario everybody trying to 'help' you actually made money and you're out $10k." / "Am I failing because I haven't bought the right course?" / "Sometimes the $500 thing is worth $500. And sometimes the $5,000 thing is something the artist could have figured out in an afternoon." [Source](https://www.hypebot.com/what-is-the-music-industrial-advice-complex/)
- Nigeria, management/team distrust (Zikoko 25 Aug 2025): "My supposed manager called me to say that I should have agreed to work with the artist without his permission." — GRNDMSTR; "Management went behind my back to talk to the investor to request more funds on my behalf." — Bizzonthetrack; "Beef isn't a growth strategy." — Mxna. [Source](https://www.zikoko.com/pop/musicians-realised-management-didnt-have-their-back/)
- Nigeria, producers (May 2026, likely sponsored): ghost producers, no contract, no refund (see section 2). [Source](https://www.pulse.ng/story/nigerian-artists-and-producers-are-both-getting-burned-graviitalbeats-is-the-saviour-2026051612234604895)
- Nigeria, a friend's judgement (Reehaa): "A friend once told me that maybe I was not hungry enough for success." (shows non-expert advice that adds self-blame). [Source](https://www.bellanaija.com/2026/05/reehaa-on-being-an-upcoming-artist-in-todays-nigerian-music-industry/)

### Inferences
- Buyers are likely wary of anything resembling "pay for exposure" or "pay for advice"; credibility signals (transparency about methods, no guarantees, small first step like a free masterclass) address documented distrust. This is inference, not sourced.
- Fineboy's "Strategy Before Streams" positioning aligns with execs' critique that artists chase numbers over strategy/storytelling.

### Gaps
- No Nigerian artist verbatim on gurus/courses/consultants. Search "Nigerian music marketing course scam" on X/Nairaland/Reddit manually.
- Quotes need re-verification from Reddit/Nairaland.
