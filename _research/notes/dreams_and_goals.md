# Dreams, Goals and Desired Outcomes of Independent Music Artists (Nigeria first; Africa and global labeled) — as of Nov 2026

Method note: ~25 searches/fetches. Quotes below are verbatim as returned by the fetch tool from the cited page (fetch-tool extractions; the page was not independently re-read in a browser). Items marked "(paraphrase)" are not quotes. Dates given where the page showed one; "undated" otherwise. I did NOT retrieve any Reddit, Nairaland, X, Instagram or YouTube-comment primary posts (search returned none usable). All "voice of the market" here comes from press interviews, which skew toward artists who already have media attention. See Gaps.

## 1. What concrete goals do artists name (stream counts, charts, playlists, shows, deals, collabs, awards, viral)?

### Takeaway
Named goals in 2025-26 Nigerian press are mostly large and global (Grammys, Billboard Hot 100, stadiums, "digital charts") rather than specific stream-count thresholds; I found NO artist quote naming "100K" or "1M" streams as a target. Concrete collab wishlists (Masterkraft, TimiJay, Don Jazzy; Wizkid, Tiwa Savage, Zlatan, Davido) and label signing appear often. Stream-count milestones are mainly framed by platforms/industry (Audiomack, Spotify), not by artists.

### Cited Findings
- Edo-state rapper Asimpleloner (9 Nov 2025): "I am hoping that in 5 years I will be selling out venues and filling out stadiums." and "I want to be one of the biggest hiphop artists from Edo state in the mold of my brothers, Rema & Shallippopi." — [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202511100653.html) (Nigeria; regionally close to the client's base in Edo)
- Seunzy (undated, approx. Aug 2025): "I am working to put out the best most sing-along records and I don't plan to stop."; goal to make music that will "dominate the digital charts, streets of Nigeria and international markets"; hopes to "pick up a Grammy in the nearest future"; wants to work with Masterkraft, TimiJay, Don Jazzy (the last three are the article's reported speech, only partially verbatim) — [Leadership](https://leadership.ng/i-will-make-the-best-records-ever-says-seunzy/)
- Kayset (undated): "I'd like to be famous and be successful in it," wants to work with "Wizkid, Tiwa Savage, Zlatan and Davido" (article text, not verbatim from artist) and "As an independent artist, Kayset said he will unveil his full potentials and is open to label signings." (article text) — [Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/news/kayset-speaks-on-next-level-ambition-in-musical-career/)
- Chizzy Abig (undated): goal to "produce sing-along rap records that are relatable so that music lovers out there can love rap music." — [Tribune Online](https://tribuneonlineng.com/my-goal-is-to-make-more-people-love-rap-music-says-chizzy-abig/amp)
- Established-but-young artists' 2026 plans: Qing Madi: "My plan for 2026 is to collaborate, brands, deals, and other cinematic ways of expression."; Wurld: "We're going crazy in 2026." (late 2025, approx.) — [Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/?p=2753104)
- Seyi Vibez (17 Sep 2026): predicts "he's gonna be a global star in the next three years" (his own prediction as quoted); "I just want to step out. I want to be different" — [OkayAfrica](https://www.okayafrica.com/exclusive-seyi-vibez-has-nigerias-attention-now-hes-coming-for-the-world/1439901)
- Ayra Starr (likely 2025; article undated, eTalk interview; star-tier, not indie, but sets the aspirational template): "I definitely want a top Hot 100 Billboard song. That's a must." / "I want Grammys upon Grammys. I'm going to tour the world." / "10 years from now, I want to do my own Super Bowl." / "I want to be big everywhere." — [Channels TV](https://www.channelstv.com/2025/07/19/i-want-a-grammy-billboard-hot-100-spot-super-bowl-says-ayra-starr/)
- Platform-side milestone framing (paraphrase): Audiomack reported "more than 58 billion Afrobeats streams in Nigeria alone since 2020", 15.3M monthly active users in Nigeria; Audiomack suits artists wanting quick discovery, Boomplay suits deep African engagement/local charts — [Techeconomy, 8 Jan 2026](https://techeconomy.ng/boomplay-vs-audiomack-african-artists-2026)
- Spotify editorial placement is a real, reachable milestone: nearly 2,000 Nigerian artists were added to Spotify editorial playlists in 2025; local artists were 80%+ of the Spotify Nigeria Daily Top 50 — [Music In Africa, 17 Mar 2026](https://www.musicinafrica.net/node/385257)

### Inferences
- The artist's own language is "global/Grammy/stadium"; the numeric "100K Audiomack streams" frame is Fineboy's framing of a bridge milestone, not (from this sample) the artist's own words. That is useful: 100K can be positioned as "proof the system works" on the way to the stated big dream.
- Collaboration with a known name (producer or star) is a recurrent wished-for milestone; this supports "who you work with" as a status marker.

### Gaps
- No verbatim artist statements about TurnTable chart, Headies, Boomplay/Audiomack Top charts, or Apple Music editorial as personal goals were found. No artist quote naming 100K/1M streams as a goal.
- No Reddit/Nairaland/X/Instagram primary posts retrieved.

## 2. Deeper dreams under the goals (full-time career, financial freedom, family, validation, legacy, going global)

### Takeaway
Under the headline goals sit three recurring motives in the sources: (1) making music a livelihood ("make music for a living"), (2) being heard/validated ("for the world to hear my story and struggles"), and (3) escape from hardship/survival mode. Going global in the mold of Rema/Asake/Wizkid-type stars is the dominant aspirational frame. Direct "take my family out of poverty" quotes were NOT found as verbatim text.

### Cited Findings
- Asimpleloner (9 Nov 2025): "I am that young man with a dream for the world to hear my story and struggles through my music."; "I grew up in the trenches so you can say I am a real trenchtown kid, but an exposed one."; "I had a sick dad so my life was basically school, home and church."; "As a child I didn't have the niceties and basic things, so I didn't have the standard life." — [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202511100653.html)
- Gida Hills (Legit.ng, 24 Sep 2022 — OLD): "That was when I knew I could make music for a living." — [Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/entertainment/celebrities/1493970-i-save-long-6-months-3-upcoming-artistes-struggles-funding-music-careers)
- Seyi Vibez (17 Sep 2026): "Even though I grow, even though I go to another level, it's still like I have to survive no matter what"; "I'm always in survival mode. I never stop"; "I know I make money with this, but I do it out of passion." — [OkayAfrica](https://www.okayafrica.com/exclusive-seyi-vibez-has-nigerias-attention-now-hes-coming-for-the-world/1439901) (shows even "made it" artists still describe survival mode)
- Shoday (undated; signed 5K Records / Sony Music UK) on identity: "I don't want to be a comedian. I don't want people to see me and laugh. I don't want to be a joke, man." and on timeline: "You can spend 10 years, then after 10 years you have your first song." — [Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/?p=2907265)
- Chizzy Abig (undated): "I just want people to understand that hard work pays no matter how long." — [Tribune Online](https://tribuneonlineng.com/my-goal-is-to-make-more-people-love-rap-music-says-chizzy-abig/amp)
- Ayra Starr: "Twenty years from now, I want to have my own brand" (legacy/brand framing) — [Channels TV](https://www.channelstv.com/2025/07/19/i-want-a-grammy-billboard-hot-100-spot-super-bowl-says-ayra-starr/)
- Independence as dignity/leverage (industry commentary, Within Nigeria, 7 Dec 2025): artists saw independence as "a way to reclaim dignity and leverage in an industry long criticized for exploitation." — [Within Nigeria](https://www.withinnigeria.com/2025/12/07/how-2025-reshaped-artist%E2%80%91label-manager-dynamics-in-nigeria/)
- WIDER/GLOBAL (not Nigerian): MIDiA (UK-centric, 6 Mar 2023): "It is about recognition, affirmation, and connection."; "Although artists want to make a comfortable living, success is not always about money."; "the underlying factor for longevity is the artist's ability to grow and maintain a core fanbase." — [MIDiA Research](https://www.midiaresearch.com/blog/the-industrys-narrative-is-not-always-the-same-as-the-artists-narrative-redefining-success-for-independent-artists)
- GLOBAL (Ditto survey, >5,000 indie musicians, 112 countries; US/UK/South Africa ~two-thirds; reported 1 Oct 2026): 74% earned under $1,000 from music last year; 64% have a job outside music; only 29% believe their music income will be sustainable over 3 years; 32% considered quitting in past 12 months — [We Rave You](https://weraveyou.com/2026/09/ditto-music-study-independent-artists-income-pain-points/)

### Inferences
- "Full-time on music" is implied (quit-the-struggle) but only one 2022 quote states it directly. The emotional core in the sample is being heard + escaping survival mode + proving hard work pays.
- Global data (32% considered quitting; 29% sustainable) suggests a latent market need for reassurance that effort leads somewhere; this is non-Nigerian evidence, so treat as directional.

### Gaps
- No verbatim "take my family out of poverty / build my mother a house / financial freedom" quotes retrieved. Likely exist on Nairaland/X/IG; not reached.
- No Nigeria-specific survey on artist aspirations found (MIDiA/Ditto/Spotify cuts are global or revenue-side).

## 3. Role models and the story artists tell about "how those artists made it"

### Takeaway
Role models cited are Rema, Shallipopi, Wizkid, Davido, Tiwa Savage, Zlatan, Masterkraft, Don Jazzy, Rihanna (Ayra Starr), The Weeknd (Seyi Vibez). The narrative implied in the press is "get a hit/viral moment, get a label or big collaborator, go global" — contested by industry commentators who say that story hides the money behind it.

### Cited Findings
- Asimpleloner: "in the mold of my brothers, Rema & Shallippopi." — [AllAfrica, 9 Nov 2025](https://allafrica.com/stories/202511100653.html)
- Ayra Starr: "I've been saying since I was 10 years old that I want to work with Rihanna." — [Channels TV](https://www.channelstv.com/2025/07/19/i-want-a-grammy-billboard-hot-100-spot-super-bowl-says-ayra-starr/)
- Seyi Vibez on his radar: "Right now, maybe The Weeknd." — [OkayAfrica, 17 Sep 2026](https://www.okayafrica.com/exclusive-seyi-vibez-has-nigerias-attention-now-hes-coming-for-the-world/1439901)
- Iyanya (X, 27 Mar 2023 — OLD, via Pulse): "When I dropped "LIKE" FT Davido and kizz Daniel, we spent 52million, song, Video and promotion." / "The cheapest I have spent on a song since January 2021 is 27m." — [Pulse Nigeria](https://www.pulse.ng/story/pulse-explainer-why-is-music-marketing-expensive-in-nigeria-2024072609264922855) (the page I fetched was dated 29 Mar 2023 by the extraction though the URL carries 2024; date discrepancy noted)
- Joey Akan (X, 28 Mar 2023 — OLD): "Everyone telling you that your song will blow "organically" on Tiktok is feeding you empty hope." and "In the first quarter of 2023, Mavin Records have spent an average of $225,000 on dropping music for artists on their roster." — same Pulse page
- Industry view on the breakout myth (Pulse, 27 Aug 2025), Marv Tomide: "The infatuation with controversy, social wins, and viral moments still trumps organic, albeit slow" — [Pulse Nigeria](https://www.pulse.ng/story/the-state-of-music-marketing-in-nigeria-so-expensive-yet-little-return-2025082718333654913)
- Label-to-independence shift: Asake's split from YBNL described as "Labels removed, the words Creative Director appeared instead." "Independence became profitable. Aggressive negotiation became normal." — [Within Nigeria, 7 Dec 2025](https://www.withinnigeria.com/2025/12/07/how-2025-reshaped-artist%E2%80%91label-manager-dynamics-in-nigeria/)
- Rema's "Calm Down" is credited to "viral moments and high-profile collaborations" (paraphrase) — [Hypebot, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.hypebot.com/african-artists-are-reaching-more-global-listeners-with-help-from-audiomack/)
- Old but relevant (Notjustok, 7 Mar 2019): "Other times, an artist could have a hit song and begin to work his or her way from there." and "The way out of the struggle most times is getting offers from record labels." — [Notjustok](https://notjustok.com/article/the-surest-way-to-blow-as-an-upcoming-artist-is-to-build-an-organic-fan-base/amp/)

### Inferences
- The "how they made it" story circulating is: hit + viral + label money + big feature. The counter-evidence (Iyanya, Akan) is that these stars' campaigns cost tens of millions of naira; copying the visible part (viral song) without the invisible part (budget/system) is the gap Fineboy can address.

### Gaps
- No direct upcoming-artist verbatim retelling of "how Burna/Wizkid/Asake made it" retrieved. Pulse 2023 items are old; a 2026 equivalent was not found.

## 5. What artists believe stands between them and the goal vs reality

### Takeaway
Stated blockers: money (funding, promotion cost), lack of support system/platform, connections/"grace", and studio/producer access. Industry reality voices say the market is saturated, paid influencer posts are losing effect, and artists chase numbers over story; "free promo" and "organic" hopes are labeled empty. Belief = "money/connections/label"; reality commentary = "saturation, strategy, fanbase, consistency".

### Cited Findings
Artist-stated beliefs/blockers:
- NAN, 14 Jun 2024 (slightly dated): Arinze Nnamdi: "We do not have a support system; you are the only one carrying out your dreams yourself." and "If there are people that could help fund musical artistes, that will be helpful"; Lilcassie: "Promotion as an independent artiste is draining."; Omotola Omodanisi: "The challenge of finance is sometimes overwhelming"; Sola Chinedu: "I currently use streaming platforms to promote my music hoping for the best."; Raymid: "It's only grace and connection that work sometimes for a person." — [NAN](https://nannews.ng/breaking-barriers-to-stardom-amid-challenges-in-music-industry/)
- Legit.ng, 24 Sep 2022 (OLD): Black Votary: "I need to get funds to promote the project." and "I can save for as long as six months just to get money to do a couple of songs."; Gida Hills: "there hasn't been a platform where people can take you on and have a plan for your career." — [Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/entertainment/celebrities/1493970-i-save-long-6-months-3-upcoming-artistes-struggles-funding-music-careers)
- Siino (Guardian, undated): "The general development of the industry is hampered by the lack of well-equipped recording studios" (via NAN piece above)
- Iyanya (X, Mar 2023 — OLD): "Promoting music has become very expensive, if you are lucky to get a song go viral make you Thank God." — [Pulse](https://www.pulse.ng/story/pulse-explainer-why-is-music-marketing-expensive-in-nigeria-2024072609264922855)
- NEC 2026 Creative Economy diagnostic (377 respondents across creative sectors, not music-only; released ~Jun 2026): affordable funding/micro-grants ranked #1 structural gap; one respondent: "Money — most of the problems I face can be resolved with extra liquidity."; distribution/monetisation named top gap — [NEC](https://nec.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/2026-Creative-Economy_Business-Infrastructure-Diagnostic-1.pdf)

Reality-side commentary:
- Pulse, 27 Aug 2025: Honour Aghedo: "The cost of breaking out is more expensive than ever. You can do everything right, and the artist won't blow up."; Dunsin Bankole: "Considering there's a lot of music coming out daily, the market is saturated, and materials get lost in the noise,"; Bankole: "The audience can now tell when an influencer is paid to post, and it's affecting how marketing campaigns convert."; Olamide Ademeso: "Artists are focused on numbers and engagement-based marketing rather than on storytelling" — [Pulse Nigeria](https://www.pulse.ng/story/the-state-of-music-marketing-in-nigeria-so-expensive-yet-little-return-2025082718333654913)
- Ovation/Mixta Marquez (date uncertain): "Stop sitting back and expecting your music to 'blow organically.'", "Please note that free promo will take you no where.", "you are not going to blow in a day, you are not going to go viral in a week or month." — [Ovation](https://www.ovationinternational.com/letter-to-upcoming-artists-by-mixta-marquez/)
- Notjustok (2019, OLD): organic fan base is "the greatest asset an artist can have" — [Notjustok](https://notjustok.com/article/the-surest-way-to-blow-as-an-upcoming-artist-is-to-build-an-organic-fan-base/amp/)
- Income reality (Spotify Loud & Clear, 2025 data, reported Mar 2026): Nigerian artists earned ~₦60bn ($43.8m) on Spotify; about ₦1.98 per stream; "One million streams earn about $300 in Nigeria" (TechCabal's calculation); 58% of royalties went to independents/labels; independent artists' Nigerian streams up 75% — [TechCabal, 16 Mar 2026](https://techcabal.com/2026/03/16/spotify-payouts-show-nigerian-artists-earned-about-₦2-per-stream-in-2025/); [Music In Africa, 17 Mar 2026](https://www.musicinafrica.net/node/385257)
- GLOBAL (Ditto, Sep/Oct 2026): stress sources: financial pressure 37%, marketing and promotion 23.5%; 82% cannot afford touring — [We Rave You](https://weraveyou.com/2026/09/ditto-music-study-independent-artists-income-pain-points/)
- Pulse (2018, OLD): "there is really no absolutely independent artist in the industry." — [Pulse](https://www.pulse.ng/entertainment/music/can-one-be-truly-successfully-independent-as-a-nigerian-artist-id8673126.html)

### Inferences
- The belief/reality gap: artists believe the blocker is money and connections (more spend, a label); commentators say spend without strategy/story/fanbase underperforms ("You can do everything right, and the artist won't blow up"). Fineboy's "strategy before streams" is aligned with the commentators' view but must acknowledge the real cost pressure.
- "Hoping for the best" (Sola Chinedu) is a direct quote that maps to the no-strategy pain.
- 1M Spotify streams ≈ $300 locally means 100K-stream milestone is about credibility/reach, not income; messaging should not promise income from streams alone.

### Gaps
- No 2026 Nigerian artist-voiced quotes on "viral song" or "better producer" as the solution. No quantitative Nigeria-only survey of what artists think blocks them.
- Spend-versus-outcome evidence for sub-₦500K budgets not found.

## 6. What help artists actively ask for (mentorship, strategy, promo, playlist, management, A&R, funding)

### Takeaway
Most frequently voiced asks: funding, a "platform with a plan for your career" (strategy/development), promotion help, a support system, recording infrastructure, and better playlist/discovery tools (global data). Management/label asks come with demands for transparency.

### Cited Findings
- Funding: Arinze Nnamdi "If there are people that could help fund musical artistes, that will be helpful" — [NAN, Jun 2024](https://nannews.ng/breaking-barriers-to-stardom-amid-challenges-in-music-industry/); Black Votary "I need to get funds to promote the project." — [Legit.ng, 2022](https://www.legit.ng/entertainment/celebrities/1493970-i-save-long-6-months-3-upcoming-artistes-struggles-funding-music-careers)
- Career planning: Gida Hills "there hasn't been a platform where people can take you on and have a plan for your career." (2022) — same Legit.ng
- Community/support: Dare Art Alade "Everybody coming together to support, give their time and helping that young artiste grow goes a long way" — NAN
- Upcoming artists need assistance (Siino, Guardian Nigeria, undated) — [Guardian](https://guardian.ng/saturday-magazine/weekend-beats/upcoming-artistes-need-assistance-to-grow-excel-says-siino/) (title only retrieved; not fetched)
- GLOBAL (Ditto 2026): 43% want better playlist/discovery tools; 41% want higher streaming royalty rates; education/resources "ranked a distant third" — [We Rave You](https://weraveyou.com/2026/09/ditto-music-study-independent-artists-income-pain-points/)
- Artist-label terms (Within Nigeria, 7 Dec 2025): artists demanded "transparency, splits that made sense, not 80-20, not opaque recoupables." and were advised to "know their numbers, understand their contracts, hire managers" — [Within Nigeria](https://www.withinnigeria.com/2025/12/07/how-2025-reshaped-artist%E2%80%91label-manager-dynamics-in-nigeria/)
- NEC 2026 (all creatives): "A community of like minds to work on projects together — without isolation, output doubles." and "Access to distribution and monetisation pathways for content is the single most urgent systemic gap." — [NEC](https://nec.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/2026-Creative-Economy_Business-Infrastructure-Diagnostic-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- Global survey: education ranks "distant third" behind tools and royalties; so a strategy/education product must be framed around outcomes (playlist/discovery, traction) rather than "learn".
- Isolation/"nobody carrying the plan with me" is a repeated emotional pain; a community/live masterclass format answers the "support system" and "isolation" language.

### Gaps
- No direct artist quote asking for "mentorship" or "strategy" in 2026 retrieved. No data on willingness to pay for guides/coaching (e.g., ₦35K, ₦99.7K price points).
- Reddit/Nairaland/X request threads not accessed.

## Source reliability notes
- Press interviews are PR-shaped; they overrepresent artists with media access. Treat as aspiration language, not representative survey data.
- Undated pages (Guardian, Tribune, Leadership partial) flagged; dates inferred only where stated.
- Pulse 2023 explainer: fetch extraction gave 29 Mar 2023 though the URL carries 2024; unresolved.
- Ovation "Letter to Upcoming Artists" shows 12 Jun 2026 but may be a re-dated older article.
- Ditto/MIDiA/NEC data are global/multi-sector; only Spotify Loud & Clear and Audiomack items are Nigeria-specific, and those are about revenue/consumption, not aspirations.
